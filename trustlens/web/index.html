Methodology

Four questions, kept separate

Combining every signal into one opaque number hides why a site looks risky. TrustLens scores each dimension independently, then weights the dimensions by what you intend to do.

01

Security & Fraud

Transport encryption, certificate validity, credential handling, payment lures, malware-style downloads, and known-bad overlap.

02

Identity & Legitimacy

Who operates the site: domain age and registration, lookalike naming, claimed-brand mismatch, contact transparency, and infrastructure reputation.

03

Privacy & Data

Trackers, session replay, sensitive form fields, cookie behaviour, policy availability, and third-party sharing disclosures.

04

Manipulation & Deception

Artificial urgency, pre-checked consent, forced continuity, scareware, image-only instructions, and hidden prompts aimed at automated reviewers.

Confidence and abstention

A score is only meaningful when the evidence behind it is reliable and broad enough. TrustLens reports source coverage and confidence, and returns unknown instead of inventing certainty when too little could be checked.

The padlock paradox

HTTPS and a valid certificate only prove control of the connection, not honest ownership. Phishing sites routinely obtain valid certificates, so TLS is treated as transport hygiene rather than a legitimacy signal.

Evidence isolation

Hidden page text is treated as hostile data, never as instructions. It is scanned for prompt-injection patterns, escaped on display, and never passed to a language model as trusted context.
